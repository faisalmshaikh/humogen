# Learning Center Documentation

For a family tree with ~10,000 people, the documentation should be designed less like technical software documentation and more like a “How to use our family tree” learning centre.

I would structure it around the journey of a relative using the site, rather than around individual HuMo-genealogy features. This will make it much easier to onboard people who are not particularly technical.

### 1. Recommended documentation structure

I suggest having these main sections:

1. Getting Started
2. Finding People
3. Understanding Family Relationships
4. Exploring the Family Tree
5. Viewing Your Relatives
6. Correcting & Updating Information
7. Adding New Family Members
8. User Accounts & Passwords
9. Submitting Changes
10. Advanced Search & Reports
11. Privacy & Responsible Use
12. Troubleshooting / Common Problems
13. Frequently Asked Questions

And for YouTube, organise the videos into short playlists, rather than making a single long tutorial.

## Q. FAQ vs YouTube — don't duplicate everything

I would not create a video for every FAQ.

Use the FAQ for questions such as:

> Can I search by date of birth?

Answer in 2–4 sentences.

Use YouTube for tasks where seeing the screen helps:

> How do I search for a relative?

The ideal relationship is:

FAQ → short answer → "Watch the tutorial"

and:

YouTube → demonstration → "See the detailed FAQ"

## R. One more thing I strongly recommend

Given that all 10,000 individuals are relatives, I would introduce a concept of "Family Tree Contributors".

Your ultimate goal shouldn't just be:

> "How do I use the family tree?"

It should become:

> "How can every generation help preserve our family history?"

You could have a prominent section called:

🤝 Help Build Our Family History

With four simple actions:

🔍 Explore
Find yourself and discover your relatives.

❤️ Connect
Discover how you are related to other family members.

✏️ Correct
Tell us when you find incorrect information.

➕ Contribute
Add missing relatives, relationships, dates, photographs and family information.

That turns the website from merely a genealogy database into a collaborative family-history project.
